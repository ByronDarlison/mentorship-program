# Chair's guide: running the mentorship program

This guide explains what you do, what the software handles, and what requires a decision.
The [Program Manual](../program/program-manual.md) contains the exact rules, questions and message templates.
Use the technical handover for setup, maintenance or a future transfer.

## The program in one minute

The program helps members make meaningful business progress through a trusted mentoring relationship.
Mentees must be current Example Chapter members. Mentors can come from any source with suitable experience.
You approve every participant and match.

The first group has no more than ten pairs. You can begin with fewer suitable pairs.
Both people attend one joint, live, one-hour online training session.
Each pair meets for one hour in ten of its twelve months.
The cycle begins with its first actual mentoring meeting, not its booking or training date.

The mentee prepares, schedules, decides and acts.
The mentor listens, asks questions, shares experience, notices possible blind spots and checks progress on prior commitments.
Both protect confidential conversations and complete quarterly check-ins.
They do not engage in paid services between each other while participating together.

## Your working tools

- **Website:** people read the program details and apply. They need no participant account.
- **Your connected AI chat:** review records, consider matches, approve changes and request reports.
- **Program mailbox:** sends approved messages and processes replies automatically on the hourly schedule.
- **Your regular inbox:** receives Chair notices and forwarded program mail when forwarding is configured.
- **Private register:** stores program status. It is not a spreadsheet you maintain manually.

Use the configured Chair connection, not an unrelated AI conversation.
Ask it to read current records before making a decision.
Do not paste participant records into public chats, GitHub issues or review documents.

## How approval works

1. Ask for the information or proposed change.
2. Read the exact proposal, affected people and any email recipients.
3. Approve it or request a change.
4. Confirm the saved result. A queued email is not yet proof of sending.

Reading records needs no change approval. Approving a participant does not approve a match or send an introduction.
The software follows your explicit approval in chat. It does not decide for you.
If details change after approval, review a fresh proposal.

The example requests below illustrate natural language. They are not special commands or approvals.
Replace descriptions with the actual person or pair in your private session.

## 1. Review applications

Start by asking:

> Show new applications and anything that needs my attention. Do not change anything.

The software saves each application, queues its receipt and notifies you of the applicant's name and role.

For each prospective mentee:

1. Read their business and matching answers.
2. Hold the short readiness conversation.
3. Confirm membership, commitments, practical scheduling and possible conflicts.
4. Decide whether to approve, wait or decline.

Review mentees first. Seek mentors whose experience fits their needs.
Have a short conversation with any prospective mentor you do not already know.
Record approval only after completing the readiness review.

> Prepare an approval for this applicant. I have completed the readiness review. Show me the proposed change.

Do not promise a place until you have approved a suitable match.
Applications stay open between groups.
For an unselected applicant, ask whether they want to remain under consideration.
There is no automatic deletion deadline for waiting applications.

## 2. Make and introduce matches

> Recommend matches from the approved applications. Show supporting experience, gaps, risks and questions. Do not approve or contact anyone.

The software prepares coded profiles for matching. Automated privacy checks can miss identifying context.
Keep confidential discussions out of application answers. Review any extract the checks refuse.

Consider each recommendation against the actual need, relevant experience, availability and conflicts.
You can choose another mentor or leave someone unmatched.

> Prepare this match for the selected group. Include my short explanation of the fit. Show the proposal before saving.

Approve the exact pair and group. The software records the match and removes temporary program-held matching copies.
Keep any external working copies out of GitHub and remove them when no longer needed.

Ask for a separate introduction draft using the manual's template.
Check the recipients and short positive explanation before approving sending.
Do not share rankings, alternatives or full applications with participants.

## 3. Arrange training and record the first booking

Choose a facilitator and a date for the joint one-hour session.
Use the [training curriculum and facilitator notes](../program/program-manual.md#required-cohort-training).
Rehearse the provisional agenda before delivery. Keep the meeting link and attendance private.

Prepare the training invitation and approve its exact wording and recipients.
After training, record actual attendance for both people in each pair.
Decide whether a live make-up is appropriate for anyone who missed it.
A pair begins only after both people complete training.

Each pair books its first 3–6 meetings before leaving training.
Record only the first planned date. The pair manages the rest of its calendar.

> Record both participants' training completion, then prepare their planned first meeting date for my approval.

The software asks the mentee for confirmation the day after that booked date.
A clear attendance reply starts the cycle using the actual date.
A rescheduling reply changes the booking and follow-up date without starting the cycle.
An unclear reply comes to you. Never assume attendance from silence or a date alone.

## 4. Follow the active relationships

The software schedules check-ins at months three, six, nine and twelve after the confirmed first meeting.
Both participants receive their own request.
They can use the private form or reply by email without entering answers twice.

| Event | What happens |
|---|---|
| No reply after seven days | The software sends a reminder. |
| Still no reply after fourteen days | It sends a second reminder. |
| No reply after twenty-one days | It flags the failure and notifies you. |
| Genuine partial reply | Reminders stop for that request, but missing answers remain visible. The deadline does not restart. |
| Missing outcome at the original deadline | That outcome counts as unsuccessful until corrected. Valid answers remain unchanged. |
| Unclear answer or low value | You review the flag and decide what to do. |
| Participant asks for contact | You arrange appropriate follow-up. |

Out-of-office replies and delivery failures do not count as participant answers.
The first-meeting confirmation follows the same reminder timing. Missing confirmation never starts a cycle.

> Show unconfirmed starts, missing answers, requests for contact, low-value feedback and unresolved delivery problems.

Review the participant's actual words before correcting a classification.
If you need to contact them, review and approve the proposed message.
Mark a flag resolved only after handling its underlying issue.
Do not clear a delivery failure merely to make the list shorter.

## 5. Handle changes and early endings

The mentee may change their business focus without asking the program to approve it.
The system does not collect the confidential agenda or track every personal commitment.

For a wrong recorded date, ask for a cycle-correction proposal.
Check its effect on scheduled requests before approval.

For an early ending, record the actual end date.
The software stops future quarterly work and replaces open quarterly requests with final feedback.
Both participants remain in the results. They do not disappear because the relationship ended early.

> Prepare an early ending for this pair on the confirmed date. Show the effect on messages and reporting before I approve.

## 6. Review results and finish the cycle

The final mentee questions ask about business progress and value.
The final mentor questions ask about value and interest in mentoring again.
The software classifies brief written answers. You resolve unclear classifications.

> Show final results with counts, percentages, pending answers and failures. Keep the four measures separate.

Targets are 80% for each of these measures:

- Mentees reporting meaningful or significant business progress.
- Mentees reporting meaningful or significant value.
- Mentors reporting meaningful or significant value.
- Mentors interested in mentoring again.

Business progress determines the mentee outcome. Enjoyment alone cannot turn failed progress into success.
Important business decisions can count. Do not require proof or an external audit.

Count each person's role in each relationship once, including early endings.
Include that role when all outcome answers arrive or its original final-response deadline expires.
Before then, incomplete outcomes remain pending. Expired missing outcomes count as failures for their measures.
Unclear and Unsure are received answers, but are not positive results.

After reviewing final feedback, ask to finish the participant's final review.
Read the deletion preview before approval.
Detailed applications and written feedback are removed from active records.
Only the approved minimum participation record remains, subject to later deletion requests.

For the Board, request an aggregate summary with counts beside percentages.
Include completion, support needs and program lessons without names or confidential stories.
Check small-group reports for details that could identify someone.

## 7. Handle privacy requests

Use the [Privacy Notice](../program/program-manual.md#privacy-confidentiality-and-deletion) for exact requirements.
Read the deletion preview before approving removal.
Resolve unclear answers when possible without delaying a valid deletion request.
Explain that erased feedback cannot be revisited.

> Prepare deletion for this person. Explain the affected records, unfinished reporting and any copies requiring separate removal.

Database deletion does not delete program email, forwarded mail, exports or Chair chat history.
Arrange separate removal of those program-controlled copies.
The connected mailbox permissions do not perform permanent email deletion.
The maintainer verifies remaining provider and recovery copies and their expiry limits.
Never confirm that every copy is gone while recoverable copies remain.

Deletion preserves overall reporting totals. An unfinished final reporting window retains only the permitted temporary private results.
After inclusion, those results become totals without an individual lookup.
A later reply cannot automatically reconnect an erased identity or change that person's totals.
Withdrawal plus deletion before any final request uses the manual's separate twenty-one-day reporting-only period.
It does not delay deletion or send further messages to the deleted person.

## 8. If the system needs attention

Ask the connected AI to read the saved status first.
If sending is uncertain, do not ask it to resend blindly.
The maintainer must check the actual mailbox receipt before retrying.
If recovery is pending, verify the previous operation stopped before approving repair.
Do not switch databases, reset dates or raise spending limits to bypass an error.

If the Chair connection is unavailable, contact the technical maintainer using the handover instructions.
Do not replace the private register with a public document or an unapproved AI session.

## Before handing over

Give the successor this guide, the manual and the technical handover.
Transfer access and retained records privately into their own accounts.
Preserve existing dates, deadlines, results and pending messages.
Verify the replacement before ending the old connection.
Only one installation should send program messages during the transition.
